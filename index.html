Living Miraculously

A Five-Day Personal Devotional

Welcome to This Devotional Journey

These five days of devotionals are drawn from the sermon "Living Miraculously." They are designed to help you move beyond simply hearing God's Word on Sunday — into a deeper, daily encounter with Him. Each day invites you to slow down, open your heart, and respond to what God is saying to you personally.

"The early church did not just believe in miracles — they lived in the constant awareness of God's presence. This devotional invites you into that same reality."

How to Use These Devotionals

🕐

Set Aside Time

Find 15–20 quiet minutes each day. Morning is ideal, but anytime works — what matters is a focused, unhurried heart.

📖

Read Slowly

Begin with the Scripture. Read it more than once. Let the words settle before moving on to the explanation and reflection.

🙏

Pray First

Before diving in, ask the Holy Spirit to speak to you. Approach each day with openness and expectation.

✍️

Journal Your Thoughts

Use the journaling space provided. Writing helps you process what God is saying and deepens your personal reflection.

💬

Discuss with Others

The discussion questions are great for sharing with your LIFE Group or a trusted friend. Faith grows in community.

📧

Share Your Reflections

You can email your journal notes to yourself for keepsake, or share them with someone who might be encouraged by your thoughts.

A Note on the Podcast Line: At the end of each day, you'll find a reminder to listen to or share the RLCC Daily Devotions podcast. This is a great companion as you go through your day.
DAY1

When Your Faith Feels Dry and Distant

Drawing Near
Today's Scripture
"Draw near to God, and he will draw near to you."
— James 4:8

This verse gives you a simple but profound invitation: come near to God. It doesn't begin with what God must do for you — it begins with your response to Him. The early church experienced awe and miracles not because they chased experiences, but because they drew near to Jesus. When you prioritize His presence over His gifts, something shifts in your heart. You begin to recognize that God Himself is the reward. And as you draw near, you become more aware of His activity in your life.

You may feel like your spiritual life has become routine or distant. Maybe you've settled for knowing about God instead of truly walking with Him. The truth is, Jesus is not far from you — you may just not be pursuing Him intentionally. What if your dryness is actually an invitation? What if God is calling you deeper, not just to receive something, but to know Him more?

  • 1What usually draws your attention more — Jesus Himself or what He can do for you?
  • 2What habits are helping or hindering your closeness with God?
  • 3When was the last time you truly felt near to God?
Set aside 10–15 minutes today to simply be with Jesus — no agenda, no requests. Just worship, listen, and draw near.

DAY2

When You're Afraid to Let Go of Control

Surrendering
Today's Scripture
"Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding."
— Proverbs 3:5

Trusting God means releasing your need to control outcomes. The early believers experienced awe because they recognized that what was happening among them was beyond human ability. That kind of life requires surrender. When you insist on controlling everything, you limit your openness to what God wants to do. But when you trust Him fully, even when things feel uncertain, you position yourself to experience His power. Surrender is not weakness — it is faith expressed in dependence.

You may be holding on tightly to something right now — your plans, your reputation, your safety. You might fear disappointment or failure if you step out. But beneath that fear is a deeper question: do you trust God enough to surrender? The life of faith will always involve risk, but it is never without God's presence. What you are afraid to release may be the very place where God wants to move.

  • 1What areas of your life are hardest for you to surrender to God?
  • 2What fears keep you from fully trusting Him?
  • 3How do you usually respond when things feel uncertain?
Identify one area where you are trying to stay in control. Pray and intentionally surrender it to God today, trusting Him with the outcome.

DAY3

When You Want to Experience God More Deeply

Reverence & Awe
Today's Scripture
"Serve the Lord with fear, and rejoice with trembling."
— Psalm 2:11

This verse captures the balance of joy and reverence. The "awe" in Acts 2:43 reflects this kind of response — a deep awareness of God's holiness and presence. When you encounter God, it is not casual; it is transformative. The early church lived with a sense that God was truly among them. That awareness shaped how they worshiped, prayed, and lived. When reverence is restored, your faith moves from routine to reality.

You may have become too familiar with spiritual things. Worship can feel ordinary, prayer predictable, and Scripture routine. But God has not changed — He is still holy, powerful, and present. The question is whether your heart is still responsive to Him. Are you approaching God with awe, or just going through the motions? When you recover reverence, you begin to experience Him more deeply.

  • 1What does "awe" or reverence toward God look like in your life?
  • 2Have you become too comfortable or casual in your relationship with God?
  • 3What helps you become more aware of God's presence?
During your next time of worship or prayer, slow down. Focus intentionally on who God is, not just what you need.

DAY4

When You Don't Know How to Step Out in Faith

Being Led by the Spirit
Today's Scripture
"For all who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God."
— Romans 8:14

Living by faith is not about guessing — it is about being led. The apostles didn't act randomly; they responded to the Spirit's prompting. Being led by the Spirit means you learn to recognize His voice and follow His direction. This requires attentiveness, sensitivity, and willingness to obey. As you grow in this, your faith becomes more active and responsive. You begin to see that God is already at work, and He invites you to participate.

You may feel unsure about how to act in faith. You might be waiting for certainty before you move. But often, God leads through quiet promptings rather than dramatic signs. Are you paying attention to His voice? Or are you too distracted or hesitant to respond? Faith grows when you take small steps of obedience.

  • 1How do you usually recognize God's leading in your life?
  • 2What makes it difficult for you to follow the Spirit's prompting?
  • 3Can you recall a time when you sensed God leading you to act?
Today, pay attention to any prompting from the Holy Spirit — no matter how small — and act on it in obedience.

DAY5

When You Feel Like Nothing Is Happening

Faithful Waiting
Today's Scripture
"Now to him who is able to do far more abundantly than all that we ask or think…"
— Ephesians 3:20

God is always at work, even when you don't see immediate results. The miracles in Acts were described as something that "was happening" — an ongoing work of God. This reminds you that God's activity is not always instant or visible. Sometimes He is preparing, shaping, and working behind the scenes. Your role is not to force results but to remain faithful. Over time, you begin to see that God has been at work all along.

You may feel discouraged because nothing seems to be happening in your life or prayers. You may wonder if God is really moving. But what if He is working in ways you cannot yet see? What if your faith is being formed in the waiting? Don't let what you don't see cause you to miss what God is doing.

  • 1When do you feel most discouraged in your faith?
  • 2How do you respond when prayers seem unanswered?
  • 3What helps you continue trusting God in the waiting?
Write down one area where you feel nothing is happening. Pray over it and choose to trust God's unseen work this week.
